# The Grey: Struggle Bus World on the whole planet

## The producer's words (2026-11-20)

The ask: "please use the read.me screenshots and general idea of the game to create a live game description i can use to better build this game. be bold and wow me with what this game could look like. i think the biggest part we're missing is a theme/premise/reason to come back. please do your best with this trailer to figure that out and paint a picture of how cool it could be. we need to figure out how to have a cool dynamic between buses/factions. right now, if you want your neighbourhood to float, you have to hit it a bunch of times, and you're essentially standing still while you're doing that so that doesn't make so much sense - its not good gameplay. idk. i think we need to get extreme here and think way outside the box and think big. any ideas?"

On the first version, which was built round San Francisco's fog: "Except your fundamental premise is wrong. This game will be built on the whole world as a whole, not just sf"

Behind it: the game's first outside player, a nine-year-old, loved shooting the other buses most and asked for upgrades and different guns (TODO.md, 2026-11-18).

## In one line

**The whole world has gone grey. Every day, crews of flying school buses paint it back, lift what they love above the night, and steal it from each other.**

Tagline: *Everybody's on the struggle bus. Ours flies.* End card: *The whole world is grey. Paint it back.*

## The four ideas

1. **One planet.** No city maps: one Earth, flown from a player's own street to orbit and down onto any street anywhere. Home is a town the player names (never read from the phone), and their floating school hangs above it.
2. **The Grey.** It rolls in with the night, on the real line between day and night, and sweeps round the planet once a day. Whatever is on the ground goes grey; only what floats survives. It is the dark, a wash of grey over the ground, never fog or cloud: the hold on weather is untouched.
3. **Loops.** The bus trails a ribbon of paint. A closed loop round real blocks bursts into colour; closed fast, it lifts. Nobody stops moving.
4. **The tail.** Lifted houses hitch to the bus on strings and are towed home to be banked. Every house on a tail is a target, and every bridge is a knife.

Four creative directors pitched blind (theme, the nine-year-old, movement, the live game); all four landed on the same core: a grey that drains the world, kids in school buses, houses that float because colour is lighter than air.

## The premise

One day the colour drained out of the whole world: cities, towns, farms, the ice. The Grey is worst at night; wherever the sun goes down, it rolls in with the dark, and grown-ups go grey and still. Kids are immune, and school-bus yellow can't be greyed, so the kids took the buses. The buses fly and barf paint. (The real world already repaints retired American school buses wild and puts them back to work: Guatemala's chicken buses, Panama's diablos rojos.) Paint is colour and colour is life ("life follows paint" already says so). Colour is also lighter than air: paint a place joyful enough and it floats above the night, where the Grey can't reach. Five crews spread across the planet, each strongest on its own kind of land.

## The sunset line: the planet's clock

- The real terminator sweeps west: about 1,670 km/h at the equator, about 1,320 km/h at San Francisco's latitude.
- Just ahead of it is golden hour: paint counts double and the lamps come on. Just behind it the Grey rolls in and takes what is on the ground.
- So the hottest place on Earth moves west round the planet once a day, a battle zone nobody schedules, met by every player at their own sunset.
- Ride the sunset: Jet (about 1,600 km/h) keeps pace with it at San Francisco's latitude; the turbo gears and Rainbow Warp outrun it.
- At night a player's town is under the Grey: fight in the sky among the floating schools, or fly west into the day.

## The planet is the board

- Any street on Earth, from the same open maps San Francisco, London and Amsterdam were baked from.
- Every place is the best in the world at something, from the map: each town has a throne (its champion bus), a route (a daily run through its own roads) and a treasure (whatever it has one of: a water tower, a grain silo, a lighthouse, a stadium, a dam).
- The land changes the game: cities are tight loops and tall prizes; farmland means loops round whole fields (crop circles of paint seen from orbit) and mile-long freight trains as the best piñatas; coasts have piers and ships; mountains canyon runs; deserts and ice are the world's sketchbook.
- The farther a tail is hauled, the more it is worth; the empty oceans between are where the computer's pirate crews wait.

## Loops

The four-hits-on-a-square rule goes.
- **The ribbon.** Flying low, the bus trails a ribbon of paint; the faster and lower, the thicker. Above about 300 m, or slower than Cruise, it paints nothing.
- **The snap.** Crossing your own ribbon closes the loop; everything inside blooms in a wave inward, with a chord and the street names.
- **A loop paints; a fast loop lifts.** Closed at Express or faster, the squares inside lift. One block at Express is about 8 s; six blocks at Jet float a neighbourhood.
- **The streak.** Each loop closed without dropping below Express makes the next bigger; a dive from height starts one; flying under a bridge refills it.
- **The tap still fires.** A Comet ahead stitches a loop's last gap; a Lob over a hill closes a loop that can't be flown.
- **Like drifting a kart.** Loops snap to the real street graph anywhere (to field edges and roads in open land); corners pull the bus in; a gap under 40 m closes itself; a ghost line shows the loop before it shuts. The 128 m squares stay the unit that lifts, as the areas engine has them.
- **The snip.** Flying through a rival's open ribbon shatters it: its paint is the snipper's, its bus floods and barrel-rolls clear.

## The tail

- **Hitch.** A lifted block's houses hitch to the bus on strings, each with its street name.
- **Bank.** Towed home to the school in the sky, they dock, safe for good; the school grows with houses from every place raided.
- **Greed.** Every house adds drag; a long tail is slow, huge and seen from far off.
- **Snap.** A shell on a tail snaps strings; loose houses drift, and the first bus through keeps them.
- **Burst.** A bus hit by a shell bursts like a piñata of paint over the blocks below, so a fight paints the place in the winner's colours; a head-on at Turbo 3 hands the whole tail to whoever hit harder.
- **Bridges are knives.** A tail that touches a tower or a cable sheds houses.
- **Nobody is knocked out.** A bus that is hit spins, is flung clear (built) and gets 3 s to run; a nearly empty bus flies fastest; the crews gang up on whoever holds the most sky.

## Crews

Each crew is strongest on its own land cover, so from orbit the planet is a war map in five colours.

| Crew | Its land | Its ribbon | Its gun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team Super Rainbow | Belongs to no country; raids everywhere | Spills colour onto the blocks next door | The Prism: one shot split five ways |
| Atlantis | Coasts, rivers, lakes | Flows downhill and paints the slopes below | The Riptide: a water cannon that cuts tails |
| The Elderwood | Forests | Vines heal small gaps; its houses are hard to tow away | The Vine: a lasso that plucks one house from 100 m |
| Hyperglow | City centres | Thinnest and fastest; glows at night, when it raids | The chrome shell: ricochets off towers |
| Inferno | Deserts, volcanoes | Burns through any rival ribbon; rams | The lava Lob: burning puddles that cut ribbons |

**Champion buses.** Each country's champion wears its own painted style, and beating it there wins the look: Guatemala's chicken bus, Manila's jeepney, Nairobi's matatu, Lagos's yellow danfo, London's double-decker, Pakistan's painted trucks, Japan's animal-shaped kindergarten buses.

**Captains who remember.** Every computer crew has captains with a name, a face on the grille and a memory: a captain who knocks you under a bridge wears your colours as a trophy decal, levels up and sends you a postcard; beaten, he comes back with a scorched hood. Beat a captain, take his gun. All on the phone, with no server.

## Guns and upgrades

The eight built shots become guns that are won; what unlocks each is the kind of building banked, read from the map's tags, so every country hands out the same guns from its own buildings.

| Bank one of these | Win |
|---|---|
| A fire station | The Hose |
| A harbour, pier or lighthouse | The Torpedo |
| A bell tower, temple or church | The Sonic Boom |
| A water tower or a grain silo | The Waterfall |
| A stadium | The Belly Flop |
| The tallest tower in a town | The Comet |
| A school | One more house on the tail |

The school in the sky grows classrooms: Shop (handling, stronger strings, a longer tail), Science (guns: two picked between trips; in flight the gear and the nose pick the shot, as today), Art (looks, the champion buses' styles). Nothing that paints, speeds or protects is ever sold.

## The reason to come back

- **Every day:** the Grey report (what survived above your town; what the other crews did overnight); your sunset (golden hour, then last call to bank); the world's hide and seek (one gold bus hidden somewhere on Earth, the same for everyone, found by clues).
- **Every week:** a wonder up for grabs (one famous place floats for the taking); crew week (one crew's Principal, a giant articulated bus, roams the planet).
- **Every term (about 6 weeks):** the planet's tally; a report card (grades in Art, PE, Music, Geography); the biggest schools sail to the Moon (the trip is built).
- **The real calendar:** Holi (a week when colour pays double), Songkran, La Tomatina, Carnival, Día de Muertos, Diwali, Lunar New Year, cherry blossom, the northern lights.
- With no server, the computer crews' moves come from the date and the hours that passed, so every phone sees the same planet. Later, a small server keeps sums by place, with no names, so the Earth from orbit shows what everyone painted.

## The share

Postcards ("I took your street. TAKE IT BACK?", for a friend in any country; the link format is built), local pride ("Our town is in it, and I'm its Top Bus"), six-second clips, and the orbit picture of the night side glittering with floating schools.

## How the whole planet gets built

Each place built in the browser as a player arrives, from the open maps (buildings, roads, places, land cover, ground heights) cut into small tiles on a host the page reads, by rules, with no hand work: the method of the project that put every building on Earth into Minecraft (TODO.md, 2026-11-17). Today `tools/bake` makes a city ahead of time from a box and a release ("City-agnostic", `overture.py`); the rules move from the bake into the page, or the bake runs over the planet into tiles. San Francisco keeps its laser-scanned detail as the showcase (`exp/ladies`). Hosting the planet's tiles is an account and a card, the producer's: a few hundred GB, likely a few dollars a month (ESTIMATE, to price).

## Three proofs first

Each on an experiment branch, each played by the nine-year-old and five or more other children. Estimates.

1. **The world** (about 2 weeks). Six very different places from open maps with no hand work (a Kansas farm town, a Phoenix suburb, Lagos, Tokyo, a fjord village in Norway, an Antarctic base), then one flight between two of them at Rainbow Warp. The test: does an ordinary place look and play like itself? It needs a download, the producer's to approve: Overture Maps' release 2026-10-21.0 for six boxes about 3 km across (ODbL), ESA WorldCover crops (CC BY 4.0, small), Copernicus GLO-30 heights for the two mountain places (free); under 1 GB together, each size printed before it is fetched. The bake's Python needs two libraries this machine lacks (`pyarrow`, `rasterio`).
2. **The loop and the tail** (2 to 3 weeks). In one of the six and in San Francisco: the street-snapped loop, the fast loop that lifts, snips, houses on strings, docking, the bridge that shears. The test: do the kids scream, and does an ordinary iPhone hold 30 fps?
3. **The Grey** (about 1 week). The sunset line on the real clock, the wash of grey behind it, the Grey report with an overnight raid. The test: do they come back the next day unasked?

## What it changes

- The world from the first version, where `CLAUDE.md`'s "The studio leads" (2026-11-14) and `STUDIO.md` say San Francisco first and the world after: the producer's word of 2026-11-20.
- "Each city is its own flat map" becomes one planet.
- The takeover rules (STAGES.md 1.3; the floating block's of 2026-11-12) go; loops and tails replace them.
- `STUDIO.md`'s spine was the postcard; it becomes the dare.
- No coins (COINS.md, GRAND-IDEA.md): houses are the currency, and they can be seen.
- A Principal is a bus, not a god, but it is a boss; the gods stay on hold.

## What could sink it

- An ordinary place built by rules that looks like nothing. Proof 1 decides it, before anything else is built on top.
- Closing loops at speed with one thumb that feels like parallel parking, and tails that stutter on an A15. Proof 2 decides them.
- Computer buses that fly like bots. The captains, and building their brains early, answer it.
- The planet's hosting and data: an account, a card and downloads, all the producer's.
