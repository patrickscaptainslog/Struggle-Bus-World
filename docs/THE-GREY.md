# The Grey: Struggle Bus World on the whole planet

## The producer's words (2026-11-20)

The ask: "please use the read.me screenshots and general idea of the game to create a live game description i can use to better build this game. be bold and wow me with what this game could look like. i think the biggest part we're missing is a theme/premise/reason to come back. please do your best with this trailer to figure that out and paint a picture of how cool it could be. we need to figure out how to have a cool dynamic between buses/factions. right now, if you want your neighbourhood to float, you have to hit it a bunch of times, and you're essentially standing still while you're doing that so that doesn't make so much sense - its not good gameplay. idk. i think we need to get extreme here and think way outside the box and think big. any ideas?"

On the first version, which was built round San Francisco's fog: "Except your fundamental premise is wrong. This game will be built on the whole world as a whole, not just sf" (recorded in `CLAUDE.md`).

On the second: "I'm thinking: you get coins from painting stuff or hitting other buses etc and then eventually need to deposit the coins in a school listed as a landmark etc. I think this is a start of a premise no? And then coins can buy upgrades and stuff"

Behind it: the game's first outside player, a nine-year-old, loved shooting the other buses most and asked for upgrades and different guns (TODO.md, 2026-11-18).

## In one line

**The whole world has gone grey. Every day, crews of flying school buses paint it for coins, shake the coins out of each other in the air, and race them to a school to bank them.**

Tagline: *Everybody's on the struggle bus. Ours flies.* End card: *The whole world is grey. Paint it back.*

## The four ideas

1. **Coins.** Painting and hitting buses fills the bus with gold. Carry it to a landmark school and hover to bank it. A hit shakes some loose for whoever grabs it first. Banked coins buy upgrades.
2. **The Grey.** It rolls in with the night, on the real line between day and night, sweeping round the planet once a day, and takes the paint back. So every morning the world is grey again and every block pays again. Schools are the one place it can't touch. It is the dark, a wash of grey over the ground, never fog or cloud: the hold on weather is untouched.
3. **One planet.** No city maps: one Earth, flown from a player's own street to orbit and down onto any street anywhere. Every town has a bank: its landmark school.
4. **Loops.** The bus trails a ribbon of paint. A closed loop round real blocks bursts into colour and pays; closed fast, it lifts. Nobody hovers and waits.

## The premise

One day the colour drained out of the whole world: cities, towns, farms, the ice. The Grey is worst at night; wherever the sun goes down, it rolls in with the dark, and grown-ups go grey and still. Kids are immune, and school-bus yellow can't be greyed, so the kids took the buses. The buses fly and barf paint. (The real world already repaints retired American school buses wild and puts them back to work: Guatemala's chicken buses, Panama's diablos rojos.) Every splash of colour a kid gives back pays in gold. The Grey can't touch gold, but other buses can. Schools are kids' ground, the one place the Grey never enters: that is where the gold is kept. Colour is also lighter than air: a block painted joyful enough floats above the night. Five crews spread across the planet, each strongest on its own kind of land.

## The coin loop

Earn, carry, risk, bank, spend. The rules, with the fixes two independent checks found (one hunted exploits; one played ten minutes as a nine-year-old and as an adult):

- **Earn.** Grey turning to colour pays, once per block per night. The money is where the buildings are (COINS.md's worth of a square from the volume of its buildings: the Financial District 21 a square, the Outer Sunset 8.5, Presidio Central 4.4), so the danger follows the money. A block that floats pays a fountain.
- **Carry.** Gold heaps on the roof and a gold number rides beside the bus. The fuller it is, the bigger and shinier the bus, and the slower it turns, with a creak.
- **Streak.** Each minute without being hit multiplies what is banked, up to three times, paid at the bank. Banking ends the streak.
- **Hit.** A shell shakes out a share of what a bus carries: 10 %, up to 30 % with the best cannon. A ram takes the same, never more than 30 %. The gold bursts into a ring that drifts toward the nearest bus for 5 s; whoever flies through it keeps it.
- **Fair.** After a hit, nothing more spills for 10 s. The same bus can't rob another again for 20 s. Nobody loses more than 30 % of a haul in a minute. A new player's first five hauls can't drop below half. A bus hunting a bounty shows as an arrow at the screen's edge 10 s before it is in range.
- **Bank.** Only landmark schools are banks (the producer's word): in a city the famous ones, about one every 2 to 3 km (from Wikidata or historic tags); in a village, its school. An arrow at the screen's edge points to the nearest and swells as the load grows. Slow to a hover inside the circle for 2 s (School Zone, built): the gold pours down, a lock clunks, BANKED 640 AT MISSION HIGH. At speed the circle is just air; nothing fires into, out of or inside it.
- **Crews own the banks.** Any bank banks for anyone, but a rival crew's school keeps a quarter as a toll, and what is banked there counts toward flipping it. A school nobody banks at for a day drifts back to neutral. The war between crews is a war over the world's banks.
- **The computer's buses play the same game.** They paint, carry real loads (scaled to the player's progress), hunt and bank; a robbed one flies off to bank; only one hunts a bounty at a time.
- **Nothing happens while the app is closed.** The bus waits where it was, load and all. Only the map moves: the Grey, and the crews' schools.
- **The first minute, scripted.** A rival knocks the coins loose; the player scoops them back; the arrow leads to a school; the player hovers, banks, and the shop opens by itself.
- **Questions players ask.** Camping by a school: a block pays once a night, and banks are kilometres apart. Hiding in Antarctica: nothing pays there and the streak clock stands still. The big city: it pays most and its crews hit hardest.

## The sunset line: the planet's clock

- The real terminator sweeps west: about 1,670 km/h at the equator, about 1,320 km/h at San Francisco's latitude.
- Just ahead of it is golden hour: paint pays double and the lamps come on. Just behind it the Grey takes the paint back, so that ground pays again tomorrow.
- So the richest place on Earth moves west round the planet once a day, a battle zone nobody schedules, met by every player at their own sunset.
- Ride the sunset: Jet (about 1,600 km/h) keeps pace with it at San Francisco's latitude; the turbo gears and Rainbow Warp outrun it.
- At night a player's town is grey and pays nothing new: hunt the buses still carrying loads, or fly west into the day.

## The planet is the board

- Any street on Earth, from the same open maps San Francisco, London and Amsterdam were baked from. Home is a town the player names, never read from the phone.
- Every place is the best in the world at something, from the map: each town has a throne (its champion bus), a route (a daily run through its own roads) and a treasure (whatever it has one of: a water tower, a grain silo, a lighthouse, a stadium, a dam), which pays like a landmark.
- The land changes the game: cities are tight loops and rich blocks; farmland means loops round whole fields (crop circles of paint seen from orbit) and mile-long freight trains as the biggest piñatas; coasts have piers and ships; mountains canyon runs; deserts and ice pay nothing and are the world's sketchbook and its hideout.
- The long haul: gold banked far from where it was earned pays more; the empty oceans between are where the computer's pirate crews wait.

## Loops

The four-hits-on-a-square rule goes.
- **The ribbon.** Flying low, the bus trails a ribbon of paint; the faster and lower, the thicker. Above about 300 m, or slower than Cruise, it paints nothing.
- **The snap.** Crossing your own ribbon closes the loop; everything inside blooms in a wave inward, with a chord, the street names and the coins.
- **A loop paints; a fast loop lifts.** Closed at Express or faster, the squares inside lift and pay a fountain. One block at Express is about 8 s; six blocks at Jet float a neighbourhood.
- **The tap still fires.** A Comet ahead stitches a loop's last gap; a Lob over a hill closes a loop that can't be flown; the tap is how buses are hit.
- **Like drifting a kart.** Loops snap to the real street graph anywhere (to field edges and roads in open land); corners pull the bus in; a gap under 40 m closes itself; a ghost line shows the loop before it shuts. The 128 m squares stay the unit that lifts, as the areas engine has them.
- **The snip.** Flying through a rival's open ribbon shatters it; its paint and its coins are the snipper's.

## Crews

Each crew is strongest on its own land cover and fights for the schools there; from orbit the planet is a war map in five colours.

| Crew | Its land | Its ribbon | Its gun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team Super Rainbow | Belongs to no country; raids everywhere | Spills colour onto the blocks next door | The Prism: one shot split five ways |
| Atlantis | Coasts, rivers, lakes | Flows downhill and paints the slopes below | The Riptide: a water cannon with a long reach |
| The Elderwood | Forests | Vines heal small gaps (the kindest for a new player) | The Vine: plucks a spill toward you from 100 m |
| Hyperglow | City centres | Thinnest and fastest; glows at night, when it hunts | The chrome shell: ricochets off towers |
| Inferno | Deserts, volcanoes | Burns through any rival ribbon; rams | The lava Lob: burning puddles that cut ribbons |

**Champion buses.** Each country's champion wears its own painted style and carries a fortune; beating it there wins the look: Guatemala's chicken bus, Manila's jeepney, Nairobi's matatu, Lagos's yellow danfo, London's double-decker, Pakistan's painted trucks, Japan's animal-shaped kindergarten buses.

**Captains who remember.** Every computer crew has captains with a name, a face on the grille and a memory: one who robs you under a bridge wears your colours as a trophy decal, levels up and sends you a postcard; beaten, he comes back with a scorched hood. Beat a captain, take his gun. All on the phone, with no server.

## What coins buy

The shop opens by itself while the bus hovers at a school; the first upgrade costs about two minutes of play.
- **Upgrades:** the cannon (shakes more loose: 10 % up to 30 %), wax (spill less), the tank (more paint), the magnet (pulls a spill from farther), the hold (the bus slows less as it fills). Looks: liveries, horns, contrails, the champion buses' styles.
- **Guns:** the eight built shots, each unlocked by painting its kind of building (read from the map's tags), then bought with coins.

| Paint one of these | Unlock |
|---|---|
| A fire station | The Hose |
| A harbour, pier or lighthouse | The Torpedo |
| A bell tower, temple or church | The Sonic Boom |
| A water tower or a grain silo | The Waterfall |
| A stadium | The Belly Flop |
| The tallest tower in a town | The Comet |

- **A sink that never runs out:** spend coins at a school to keep its neighbourhood in colour through one night's Grey.
- Nothing that paints, speeds or protects is ever sold for money; coins are earned, never bought; later, money buys looks only.

## The reason to come back

- **Every day:** a fresh world (the Grey took the paint back, so every block pays again); the Grey report (which schools the crew holds, which the others flipped overnight); your sunset (golden hour pays double); the gold bus (one huge piñata hidden somewhere on Earth, the same for everyone, found by clues).
- **Every week:** a wonder that pays ten times over; crew week (one crew's Principal, a giant articulated bus carrying a fortune, roams the planet).
- **Every term (about 6 weeks):** the planet's tally of banks; a report card (Art, PE, Maths, Geography); the finale: the Moon's only bank opens at a landing site (its circle stays unpainted, as the ruling on crewed landing sites says).
- **The real calendar:** Holi (a week when colour pays double), Songkran, La Tomatina, Carnival, Día de Muertos, Diwali, Lunar New Year, cherry blossom, the northern lights.
- With no server, the computer crews' moves come from the date and the hours that passed, so every phone sees the same planet. Later, a small server keeps sums by place, with no names.

## The share

"I'm carrying 2,000 coins over your street. Come and get me": the link lands a friend in any country right there, against the sender's bus flown by the computer, with the coin-taking upgrades evened out to the weaker bus (the link format is built). Local pride ("Our town's school is ours, and I'm its Top Bus"). The report card. Six-second clips of gold spraying under a famous bridge.

## How the whole planet gets built

Each place built in the browser as a player arrives, from the open maps (buildings, roads, places, schools, land cover, ground heights) cut into small tiles on a host the page reads, by rules, with no hand work: the method of the project that put every building on Earth into Minecraft (TODO.md, 2026-11-17). Today `tools/bake` makes a city ahead of time from a box and a release (`overture.py`); the rules move from the bake into the page, or the bake runs over the planet into tiles. San Francisco keeps its laser-scanned detail as the showcase (`exp/ladies`). Hosting the planet's tiles is an account and a card, the producer's: a few hundred GB, likely a few dollars a month (ESTIMATE, to price).

## Four proofs first

Each on an experiment branch, each played by the nine-year-old and five or more other children. Estimates.

1. **The coin loop** (about 2 weeks). In San Francisco against the computer's crews: downtown is the big city, the Sunset the small town, the ocean the empty world. Coins from paint and hits, the spill and its fairness rules, landmark schools as banks, the scripted first minute, three upgrades. The test: do they play ten minutes unasked, and understand "gold on the bus can be taken, gold at school is safe" with no words? No download.
2. **Loops** (1 to 2 weeks). The ribbon, the street-snapped loop, the fast loop that lifts, snips. The test: does painting at speed feel like drifting a kart?
3. **The world** (about 2 weeks). Six very different places from open maps with no hand work (a Kansas farm town, a Phoenix suburb, Lagos, Tokyo, a fjord village in Norway, an Antarctic base), each with its landmark schools. The test: does an ordinary place look and play like itself? It needs a download, the producer's to approve: Overture Maps' release 2026-10-21.0 for six boxes about 3 km across (ODbL), ESA WorldCover crops (CC BY 4.0, small), Copernicus GLO-30 heights for the two mountain places (free); under 1 GB together, each size printed before it is fetched. The bake's Python needs two libraries this machine lacks (`pyarrow`, `rasterio`).
4. **The Grey** (about 1 week). The sunset line on the real clock, the paint taken back behind it, the Grey report. The test: do they come back the next day unasked?

## What it changes

- Coins come back, where `STUDIO.md` part 6 cut "the coins and store plan" (2026-11-14). A hit takes a share of what a bus carries, where COINS.md said a hit never takes coins. Coins buy upgrades (power), never with money; nothing is ever sold for money that paints, speeds or protects.
- A gold number rides beside the bus while flying, where COINS.md said no number while flying.
- The world from the first version (the producer's word of 2026-11-20); "each city is its own flat map" becomes one planet.
- The takeover rules (STAGES.md 1.3; the floating block's of 2026-11-12) go; loops replace them.
- `STUDIO.md`'s spine was the postcard; it becomes the dare.
- A Principal is a bus, not a god, but it is a boss; the gods stay on hold.
- The children's questions COINS.md lists for the lawyer stand, with one more: a child's home town and school must never leave the phone.

## What could sink it

- Rival buses that rob a child blind: the fairness rules are the answer, and proof 1 tests them on children.
- An ordinary place built by rules that looks like nothing: proof 3 decides it.
- Closing loops at speed with one thumb that feels like parallel parking: proof 2 decides it.
- Computer buses that fly like bots: the captains, and building their brains early.
- The planet's hosting and data: an account, a card and downloads, all the producer's.
