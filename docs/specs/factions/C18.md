## 1.5 The neighbourhoods (new, after 1.4)

**What the world gives** (Proposed). `public/city/<slug>/hoods.bin` is one file a city, gzipped like the tiles. Its byte layout is the world's, written beside the parser in `src/world/hoods.ts`, as `tiles.ts` does for the tiles. What the areas code against is the parsed shape:

```ts
export const HOODS_VERSION = 1;
export interface NeighbourhoodTable {   // feat/recess ab6c67c, src/factions/neighbourhoods.ts, field for field
  names: string[];
  hearts: number[];                     // each one's heart, a square's key
  squares: number[][];                  // each one's squares, keys
  tagged?: Iterable<number>;            // squares tagged worship, cemetery, memorial, school or hospital: stages 4 and 5 stay off
  godless?: boolean[];                  // too small for a god, or no square for a heart: squares only (Alcatraz)
}
export interface HoodsTable extends NeighbourhoodTable { version: number; minor: number; tagged: number[]; godless: boolean[] }
export function parseHoods(buf: ArrayBuffer): HoodsTable | null;   // the inflated file; null for one it can't read, never a throw
export function loadHoods(base: string, ref: HoodsRef | undefined, query?: string): Promise<HoodsTable | null>;
```

- The parser is worker-safe: no three.js and no DOM. `query` is the caller's `dataQuery()`, so the parser doesn't import `City.ts`.
- city.json gains `"hoods": { "version": 1, "minor": 0, "bytes", "count", "squares", "godless", "tagged" }`. A city without it has no file, and the areas keep their stand-in there.
- Nothing in the game imports the loader yet, so no page fetches the file. The first caller is behind a flag, as `?recess` is.

**What the world guarantees** (Proposed; W7 counts each):
- **The squares** are the areas' own, 128 m on the map's origin, keyed `packSquare(ix, iz)` (`areas.ts`).
  - A square is land when half its 2 m ground texels in the baked tiles aren't WATER, inside city.json's `bounds`.
  - Every land square is in exactly one neighbourhood, and no other square is in one.
  - Squares past the frame (the tiles that only hold buildings crossing its edge), and squares mostly water (a pier's end), give `of(key)` -1, as water does.
- **No neighbourhood crosses water.** Land squares that touch, corners included, are one piece of land.
  - A piece of land with no seed of its own gets one neighbourhood, named for its islands: Treasure Island and Yerba Buena Island.
  - A whole piece of land under 0.25 km² keeps its own neighbourhood, godless: Alcatraz Island is 4 squares.
- **Sizes.** Every neighbourhood is 0.25-3 km² (16-183 squares), except a whole piece of land under it.
  - The areas' `km2(n)` is `squares.length` times 0.016384, as it already is.
- **Hearts.**
  - Each heart is one of its own neighbourhood's squares, and never a tagged one.
  - Hearts of neighbourhoods with a god stand at least 450 m apart, square centre to square centre.
  - A heart is in the neighbourhood's biggest park, on a square the park covers a quarter of that is open ground (grass, plaza, path, sand, forest or rock on a quarter of the square: a park on a roof isn't). With no such park it's the highest open ground, else the highest square.
  - Within a park or a tier: first a ring of 8 with no tagged square in it, then the ring most on land, then the one most its own, then a square with no tagged place in it at all (a memorial's point included).
  - A square whose ring reaches past the frame, or that is a quarter water or more, comes only after every other.
- **Rings.** Each god's ring of 8 is its own wherever it's land, so the areas' `ring()` is the whole ring there. A neighbour's land square in it came over in the bake, unless a park holds it (parks stay whole), it's another heart or in another heart's ring, or the neighbour couldn't spare it (it would fall under 0.25 km², or in two) or the god would go over 3 km².
  - A ring is short of 8 only for water, the frame's edge or one of those squares. W7 reports each short ring and why.
- **Godless.** `godless[n]` is true for a whole piece of land under 0.25 km², or for a neighbourhood whose every square is tagged. The areas' own `godless(n)` still adds its tests: under `godMin`, or a tagged heart.
- **Tagged.** A square is tagged when worship, cemetery, memorial, school or hospital, as Overture maps them in its land use, buildings, land and infrastructure, cover a quarter of its land.
- **Parks are never cut in two.** A park's squares, those it covers a quarter of that no bigger park holds, are one neighbourhood's on each piece of land.
  - The one exception is a park of more than 45 squares (0.74 km²), which a split over 3 km² can cut: the Presidio is in three, and Golden Gate Park in two.
  - A park named for a neighbourhood it lies in or touches is that one's, the longest name first: Mission Dolores Park is the Mission's.
- **Outlines.** A drawn outline holds its own place and the places drawn as points inside it, each square to the nearest of them: Japantown, the Fillmore and Lower Pacific Heights are in the Western Addition's outline. A place with an outline of its own holds no square past it. A square in no outline goes to the nearest point in none.
- **Names.** There's one list, used everywhere.
  - No two names are one place: case, accents, punctuation, spaces, "St." for "Saint", and "the", "district" and "neighbo(u)rhood" don't count.
  - A merged neighbourhood keeps both names, as "A / B", and two at most. A name with a slash of its own is never joined to another.
  - Every place the bake seeds from is named on the neighbourhood holding its point, or left off only where that one shows two names already. A place left with no square of its own rides with the neighbourhood holding its point: Tenderloin / Little Saigon.
  - A place mostly off the map (under a quarter of its outline in the frame, and under 0.25 km² of land on it) names nothing on it.
  - Names come from Overture alone, never a hand-placed list. Each is a place's own name as the map has it, a division's, a park's, a land feature's or a street's, or one made from them by a rule: a split's strips (Presidio West), and an umbrella's middle. A suburb's point with two parts named round it, Inner and Outer Sunset, isn't a neighbourhood beside them. Its own ground is Central Sunset, or nothing where a Central one is mapped.
  - The street sign still reads the bake's old per-tile names until it moves to this list. That move is a change on screen, the producer's to see.
- **Inputs.** A city with no Overture extract stops the bake with exit 1, and nothing is written.

**Ids and versions** (Proposed):
- A neighbourhood's index `n` belongs to its file alone, with the names sorted. A re-bake can renumber them, and nothing saved or shared keys on one.
- What's saved keys on squares, as the areas' save already does (16 bytes a square). A god is saved as stage 5 on its heart square.
- A square's key never changes, because it's the map's grid. A heart's key does change when its heart moves.
- The file starts `'SBN1'`, then a u16 major and a u16 minor.
  - A minor adds what old readers can skip, and never moves anything.
  - A major is a break, and `loadHoods` gives null for a major it doesn't know, so the areas run on their stand-in and the page goes on.
- Files under `/city/` take `?v=` as every city URL does. `__sbDataV` is bumped in the commit that changes a `hoods.bin` on its way to main: osm7 to osm9 at 61ef776 covers this branch's re-bakes, none of them on main yet.

**When it changes** (Proposed):
- A city's `hoods.bin` changes only when `tools/bake/hoods.py <slug>` reruns. That happens on a city's re-bake (its land), a new Overture release, or a change to the rules here.
- A rerun on the same inputs writes the same bytes (W7).
- Every re-bake's commit names the neighbourhoods added, dropped or renamed, the hearts that moved and how many squares changed neighbourhood. `hoods.py --json` holds each one's sources and heart for that diff.
- How the areas read a save across a change is the areas' call, in their own spec. The world's proposal is short:
  - a square keeps its owner and stage;
  - a stage-5 square that is no longer a heart comes back at the Lift (4);
  - a new heart starts with no god.

## 7. W7, for the tests at each edge (the world's)

- **W7** `python -I tools/bake/hoodcheck.py <city> --twice --broken` (Python, the bake's venv). It holds the file to the city's baked tiles and Overture extract, and checks every guarantee of 1.5 above, each counted.
  - `--twice` runs the bake twice for the same bytes.
  - `--broken` breaks a copy on purpose for each check, and each copy has to fail it. It also bakes the city against an empty extract, which has to stop with exit 1 and write nothing.
- `node tools/world/hoods-check.mjs` (Node) reads the files with the shipped loader, through `fetch` and gzip.
  - The table's digest equals hoodcheck's for the Python reading.
  - Each of seven broken copies comes back null, never a throw.
- Where they run:
  - `hoods-check.mjs` reads only committed files, so it runs anywhere, the cloud machine included.
  - `hoodcheck.py` needs the city's Overture extract, which lives in the bake's cache and not in git. Without it, it stops with exit 1. It runs where the city was baked, and the bake's log names the libraries the bytes came from.
