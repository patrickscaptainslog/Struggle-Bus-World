## The worker

A module worker, as `CONTRACT.md` section 2 says: `postMessage` and transfer lists only.

- **In:** `init` (the place's slug and origin, the relay's address or none, the weather, the place's time zone, and its road graphs: a region's URL, the regions `city.json`'s `life.regions` lists, a region's side), `lawn` (a tile's grass for people, below), then `tick` at 10 Hz of the page's clock: `utcMs`, the bus's place on the map (x, y, z) and in degrees (for the relay), and the radius to fill: 80 km from the street, the horizon from the flight. The worker asks the relay itself, every 10 s, and fetches the road graphs itself, below.
- **Out:** a snapshot, transferred, which the host sends back with a later `tick` to be filled again: the same two buffers all night (`smoke.mjs` counts them). What a snapshot's arrival makes is its views over the new buffer, about 1.2 KB at 10 Hz (`alloc.mjs` prints it): a transfer arrives as a new `ArrayBuffer`, and with no shared memory there's no other way to read one.
- **A row** is 16 floats: the mover's key hash (u32 bits), kind, x, y, z at the snapshot's `utcMs`, vx, vy, vz (m/s), heading, pitch, bank (rad), size scale, flags (live), the seconds its paint would last from then (`until`), and two spare. The host carries a mover on between snapshots by its velocity, so a 10 Hz worker draws at 30 or 60 fps with no step.
- **Keys** are `CONTRACT.md` section 3's. The worker hashes them for the row (FNV-1a), and keeps the strings for the movers in reach, so the host can ask for a key by its hash when a breath lands.

## How movers are drawn

Three pools, one draw each in the scene pass, one geometry each holding every kind in its pool: each vertex says which kind it belongs to, and the vertex shader folds away what isn't the instance's (scale 0). So cars and small planes share the middle pool's one draw, and ferries, sailboats and airliners the large pool's: a pool's every instance draws every kind's boxes, folded or not, and the large pool's instances are few, so a kind's boxes cost least there (`LIVING.md` put boats in the middle pool). A pool's model is a few dozen triangles a kind, made for pixel art at 6 to 40 px. Under 6 px the same model, which reads as a few pixels anyway; under 2 px a speck, one point in the specks' draw (`LIVING.md` rule 4: never a draw for something under two pixels). Painted movers keep a 1 px floor to orbit (`CONTRACT.md` 1.4).

Per instance: its matrix, its ramp (the grey world's rows, or its faction's look's body painted, `HUE_RAMPS[hue]` for Rainbow), its level (1 to 3), a glint (the aim's, a step up, as on buildings) and a surface id. A model's boxes can be lamps. At night, when the windows light (the live sky's and the held hour's `uLit`), a grey plane's lamp is its beacon, on the fin of its model and as its speck: the lamps' ramp an eighth of every 1.2 s, each its own phase from its key; a painted one keeps its paint (the first account's GPU review, n1: a model showed none). A car's lamps are its headlights, on at night grey or painted; under 14 px, where its model's lamps are too small to see, a speck at its nose is them, which its body hides from behind; a car's speck is steady, and drawn at night down to 0.3 px where by day it goes under 1. Each pool's material ends in `sbWrite`, flags 1.0 (outlined); specks and marks 0.5. All of them are registered with the warm-up (`registerWarm('life', ...)`), so nothing compiles in flight.

The pools are made at their largest at boot (`CONTRACT.md` section 2: people 4,096, middle 2,048, large 512, specks 16,384) and drawn with `count` set to what's live, one upload of the live range an attribute a frame, its range object reused (three's `addUpdateRange` makes one a call).

**Draws.** Six in all, each only while something in it is live: four pools, the specks and the marks (the first account's GPU review, m2: `LIVING.md`'s three draws were the pools; the fourth, the heavy pool, is for the buses and trucks: in the middle pool every car would draw their boxes). By the code, a pool's instance draws every box of every kind in it, those not its own folded to a point: a picnic or a dancer 23 boxes, 276 triangles, a picnic's 36 to 180 of them shown; a car or a small plane 13, 156, a car's 84 of them shown; an airliner, a ferry or a sailboat 20, 240; a bus or a truck 22, 264, 84 to 108 shown. So 600 cars and 100 buses and trucks at a morning peak are about 120 K triangles sent and 60 K drawn, and 1,200 picnics on a Saturday afternoon (the most the worker fills) 330 K sent, against `LIVING.md`'s 80 K for the whole of life (ESTIMATE). Not yet measured on any GPU: L3.

**Marks** (`CONTRACT.md` O6): one draw of ribbons, 8,192 vertices, 12 points a ribbon. A painted plane's contrail runs back along its path from its tail for as long as it has flown since the hit, 40 s of it at most, turned to the lens, a pixel wider a level up (2, 3, 4 px, and 8 m at the least), on its paint's ramp; at level 3 it runs through the hues. It fades as paint can in an indexed G-buffer, its pixels dropping out by a 4 x 4 ordered dither toward the old end, its first third solid, so the newest paint is the loudest. Flags 0.5, no shadow, fog cut as a painted speck's. A painted car's tyre tracks are two of them laid flat on the road from its rear axle back, either side of its middle, 0.32 m wide at the least and a pixel at the most, 0.12 m over the road: where it has been, a point every 2.2 m kept by the host for 48 cars at once (the snapshot has no path), each stretch its own hue round all six, the hue it was laid in, so the rainbow stays on the road where it fell and the oldest end dithers away first. A painted boat's wake is a contrail laid flat on the water instead, 0.15 m over it, from its stern back for as far as it has gone since the hit and 40 s of it at most: 30 % of the boat's length wide at the stern and spreading to three times that, in every hue at any level.

**From the flight's loop** (`CONTRACT.md` O4). Over 15 km the flight has the frame, and its own scene and render frame: the bus at the origin, a unit a metre, its axes in J2000 from Earth's centre. `moonrun.ts` hands life the bus Earth fixed with the render axes in that frame (`frameSky(dt, utcMs, ob)`), and the pools move into the flight's scene while it has the bus, back into the street's when the street has it. Each row is put Earth fixed once a snapshot, exactly (the map's projection, then the ellipsoid), and carried on by its velocity there; none is drawn behind the Earth (the line from the bus passes inside the ellipsoid); past 25 km a mover is drawn nearer the lens on its own line of sight, its size cut to match, since the flight draws to 30 km. A plane at the hand-over is where the frames before said it would be (`sky.mjs`). Not so a mover on the ground (open, 2026-11-05): two ferries tied up at Oyster Point, 12 km off, jump 7.9 px on the flight's first frame where the same guess between the street's own frames misses by 0.09 px; every plane holds under 6. Not found yet: the flight's lens against the street's at the hand-over, or the street's flat map against the ellipsoid, both of which show most on what's near and low. `sky.mjs` prints it and fails on planes alone. Off the map's sky, from about 400 km out, the projection is past what it's good for, and the planet's own sky is a later stage.

## Cars

`src/life/sim/cars.ts`, on the world's road graphs (`CONTRACT.md` section 1.2, `GRAPHS.md`). With the bus under 2,500 m the worker fetches the graph of each region within 2.1 km of it, two at a time, nearest first, inflates and parses it (`lifeData.ts`'s `parseRegionGraph`), and drops those past 3.5 km; each time the regions loaded change it builds the net cars drive, in the regions' order so every player's is the same (50 to 80 ms in Node for 22 to 24 regions round downtown). A way of the net is one direction an edge is driven: roads only, never `UNDRAWN` (the decks the first account is building, which stay hidden until they land and the graphs are baked again), `NO_CARS` or a track. The ways on from each way's end are the ways from that node, a portal's two copies one node, in the edges' id order, never back the way it came; straight on is weighted five times a turn, and a turn back past 120 degrees is all but never taken unless it's the only way on.

Each way has a stream of slots, its headway at the busiest hour its class's cars a km each way (motorway 36, trunk 28, primary 22, secondary 16, tertiary 11, residential 5, unclassified 4, living street 1.5, service 1.2, ESTIMATE) over its length and a car's mean life, 2 s at the least. A slot's car is born at the way's start at a hashed time in the first half of its slot, if the local hour's traffic says so (a share of the busiest, read between the hours: 0.1 at 3 am, 1 at 8 am and 5 pm, the city's zone from `city.json`'s `tz` through `Intl` once a minute), lives 45 to 90 s, and drives a hashed path from there, each way at its own speed: the graph's, 85 % of it, 95 % on a motorway, else its class's (88 km/h on a motorway to 26 on a residential street and 12 in a service lane, stops and lights in it). The graphs have no lanes yet, so a class's: three on a motorway, two on a trunk or primary, one else, 3.2 m each, right of the line on a two-way road and across it on a one-way. Its place at `utcMs` is its path walked for its age: a closed form, so ten minutes' steps end where one jump does (`determinism.mjs`).

A car is placed on the chord between the points 4 m behind and 4 m ahead of it along its path, at its middle, facing along it, its lane's offset the mean along the chord: so it takes a bend round rather than through its corner, a short stretch of a way's line or a region's split changes nothing, and a lane's change between two ways is a slide over 8 m. Its offset is as much of itself as the chord is straight, so round a hairpin it keeps to the line. Nothing jumps or snaps: at a 20 ms step the worst was 0.26 m past its speed and 0.09 rad a step over 469,000 pairs. It grows in and out over 1.2 s where it's born and where its life or its path ends (a dead end, or the regions loaded); in a tunnel it isn't drawn, so it goes into the hill and comes out of it. The worker fills every car within 700 m of the bus and fewer on out to none at 1.2 km (3.5 px on the portrait lens), each by its own hash, so the traffic thins into the distance and a car appears as the bus comes on; all of them under 1,500 m, fewer as the bus climbs to none at 2,500. At 09:30 round the Financial District that's 600 cars, at 3 am 60; a tick of them 2.4 to 5.4 ms in Node here. Keyed `car:<edge id>:<direction>:<slot>`.

What it is comes from its slot's hash and its birth road's class (the producer, 2026-11-05: "where are all the buses? There are a lot of buses in sf, and different sized cars - trucks, suvs, semis etc"): on a motorway or trunk 48 % sedans, 26 % SUVs, 8 % pickups, 7 % box trucks, 8 % semis, 3 % buses; on a primary or secondary road 50, 24, 6, 7, 2 and 11 %; on a tertiary 54, 25, 7, 5, 1 and 8 %; on the rest 60, 27, 8, 4, none and 1 %. Round the Financial District at 09:30 that's a bus one vehicle in ten on the arterials (`determinism.mjs`: 9.8 %). Its tone is the street's own greys, so it stands out from the road: a car white, silver, grey or black at 30, 30, 20 and 20 %, a box truck white or silver, a semi mostly white, a bus white. A little over life size ("maybe make the cars slightly larger than reality for starters"): a sedan 5.7 m long, an SUV 6.2, a pickup 6.4, a bus 13.8, a box truck 10.2, a semi 23.8. The big ones keep to the kerb's lane, and a slot whose vehicle would be born on top of the one before it at the way's start isn't born.

Cars are drawn from the middle pool: an axle's pair of dark tyres front and back, the body, and by its look a sedan's cabin, an SUV's taller and longer one or a pickup's cab forward of its bed, of glass (two steps darker by day), and two headlights; the big ones from the heavy pool, a bus with its windows down either side and its windscreen, glass that lights at night, a box truck's cab and box, a semi's tractor and trailer. Each pitches with the road. Painted, a vehicle keeps the paint for the session only, and comes alive: it bounces as it drives, about 2.2 hops a second and 0.3 m high on its own beat, squashed as it lands and stretched as it leaves, its nose nodding and its body rocking, and lays its rainbow tyre tracks (Marks, above). The honk in the chord `LIVING.md` names isn't in yet, and buses run on the arterials by share, not on Muni's routes, which would need the world's files to carry them (OSM's bus route relations): for the first account. A vehicle is taken on the line of fire alone, as a picnic is, within its cone (Paint on a mover, below).

## Ferries and sailboats

`src/life/sim/boats.ts`, on `routes.bin` and the graphs' open water (`CONTRACT.md` section 1.2). The worker fetches `routes.bin` once.

**Ferries**, on every route in it, a route listed both ways only once: San Francisco's 20 are 19 lines, 26 vessels. A timetable made from the route, "very educated guess simulated" (the producer): from 06:00 to 22:00 local, a vessel every half hour on a run under 5 km and every hour on a longer one, as many vessels as a round trip takes; each crossing at its run's speed (7 m/s under 5 km, 12 to 15 km, 15 over), a minute and a half to get up to it and to slow. A round trip is exactly its vessels times its headway, the slack spent at the berths (8 minutes at the least), so a line's vessels keep their headway; each line's first sailing is chosen, in id order, as far as it can be from the other lines' sailings at both its terminals. At a terminal two or more lines share (ends within 120 m), each line has its berth along the terminal's axis, 40 m apart, either side in turn, and its own vessels lie side by side 12 m apart; it slides into its berth over its last 500 m in and out over its first 500 m out. They're double-ended, as many on the bay are, and never turn round, so none sweeps the one beside it. Velocity is from the ferry's own place a second on. Over a day no two hulls (40 by 10.4 m) touch at rest, and one passes another at its berth for a minute, where before 1,396 minutes of the day had two ferries within 40 m (`determinism.mjs`; the first account's life review, M1). Every ferry is in the snapshot whatever the bus's height, at the water's height; a ferry is 2 px at about 19 km. Keyed `ferry:<route>:<vessel>:<service day, YYYYMMDD>` and kept to that day's last crossing, as section 3 has it (M3). Drawn from the large pool: the hull, two decks of cabin, the wheelhouse on top in the middle, each deck's windows a lamp along either side, lit at night grey or painted; its speck is its lights.

**Sailboats**, out of each marina in `routes.bin` by day, more at weekends: a slot for every ten berths (111 in San Francisco's 9 marinas), every one out on a Saturday and 45 % on a weekday, from a hashed time after 09:30 for two to five hours, home by 19:00, growing in and out over a minute at each end (27 out at 14:00 on a Thursday, 65 on a Saturday). Each slot sails a loop off its marina, an ellipse 250 m to 1 km out, 120 to 500 m long, tried from hashed ones until one is all open water with a margin: the graphs' `OPEN_WATER` triangles of every region within 1.4 km of the marina laid on a 20 m grid, a point water only if its cell and the eight round it are, so every player's loop is the same once those regions are in (85 of the 111 find one). Round it at 2.8 to 4.2 m/s, heeled away from the bay's westerly (its mast leaning downwind: the review's m3 found it leaning upwind), the most with the wind on her beam. A marina is sailed while the bus is within 6 km of it and under 6 km up, and its boats filled to 5 km round the bus; its regions are fetched for its water whether or not cars want them. Keyed `sail:<marina key>:<slot>:<YYYYMMDD>`, kept till it's home (section 3). Home by sunset at the latest, the place's own, from the Sun's declination and the equation of time by the date (`sim/sun.ts`; the review's m7). Drawn from the large pool: the hull, its cabin, the mast, the mainsail and the jib.

Painted, a boat rides a swell, about once in 1.6 s, its bow and sides rising and falling in turn, a metre a 40 m of it, and lays its rainbow wake (Marks, above). A boat can be had three ways now: a tap's circle (`life.blast`), a still hold on it (`Lock.ts`, SHOTS.md's lock-on: 1.5 km for a boat) and the Torpedo, a shot that comes down on open water within 300 m of it (`Torpedo.ts`; HOOKS.md, "The Torpedo"): the breath runs along the surface at the boat at 130 m/s with spray at its head and a wake of its own, or skips twice where no boat is in reach. A locked shot's hit or a Torpedo's is an aimed hit: for 30 s the boat's wake is the one colour and twice as wide, easing back over its last 8 s. Not yet: a ferry's horn in the progression, its rainbow decks a hue a deck, a sailboat's rainbow sails and the regatta a painted bay starts, all `LIVING.md`'s; and a boat's way from its berth to its loop. Boats are taken by the aim's pick radius, as planes are, not on the line of fire alone.

## Planes

**Live**, through the relay: the game asks `/api/planes?lat=&lon=&r=` (a box round the bus, in nautical miles as adsb.lol takes it) every 10 s while the street or the flight is over Earth. The relay asks adsb.lol's `v2/point` and keeps the answer at the edge for 10 s (`Cache-Control: s-maxage=10`), so a thousand players over one city make one request upstream. What it passes on, per aircraft: hex, flight, type, lat, lon, `alt_geom` (else `alt_baro`), ground speed, track, vertical rate, category, and whether it's on the ground. Only airline flights are named (`LIVING.md` rule 7: a call sign of three letters and a number); the rest go anonymous. A live plane is carried on from its report by its speed and track, up to 30 s, then dropped if no report has come.

**Scripted**, when the relay is down or not deployed (a page on localhost, a pinned or stepped clock, the tools): flights straight across a disc of 150 km round the place, at cruise (9.5-12 km, 60 %) or a middle height (3-7 km), two slots a UTC minute flown seven times in ten, deterministic by place and minute, no airport needed: 22 to 39 up at once over San Francisco across a day, 30 on average (`determinism.mjs`). Few cross the city itself (one in an hour over its middle on the 5th's evening): approaches and departures on each airport's real runways come with `airports.bin` (`CONTRACT.md` O11) and fill the city's sky. Nothing on the screen says which a plane is.

**The ribbon.** A plane hit by a locked shot (SHOTS.md, "Lock-on": "a ribbon of colour across the sky, seen from the whole city") trails a ribbon of the one colour for 60 s: 60 m wide and never under 4 px, as long as the plane has flown since the hit (2.4 km at 50 s for a small plane at 60 m/s), then easing back over 8 s to the usual contrail, 40 s long and 8 m wide. `tools/life/torpedo.mjs` reads its length and width at 30, 50, 56 and 70 s.

**From the street** at 15 km and under, an airliner is about 70 m and makes 6 px at about 13 km on the portrait lens (`CONTRACT.md` 1.4's arithmetic), so most planes in a San Francisco sky are specks and the ones on approach to SFO models.

## Paint on a mover

## Curb's road side (the shots' plan, 2026-11-06)

`docs/specs/shots/SHOTS.md`, "Curb: cruising beside the cars": the bus lands on the street and rolls beside the cars with no steering. The road side is life's; the flight, the camera and the landing are the first account's, and call it.

- **The line** (`sim/curb.ts`, in the worker, on the cars' own net). `curbLand` finds the bus's line of the nearest way driven the bus's way within 80 m: a way cars may drive, never a tunnel, an alley or a living street. The line is the kerb lane's (the right-most) moved `curbTune.out`, 3.9 m, toward the kerb. The bus is 4 m wide at its scale and a truck 3 m at its, so a truck in the kerb lane passes 0.4 m off it and a car 0.6 to 0.7 m: on a street with one lane each way, every car going the bus's way rides beside it, never inside it (the day review's B2). There the bus's side is 7.5 m from the street's middle, so a kerb drawn for Curb stands there or further out. `curbPath` gives that line ahead, a point every 4 m with the road's speed; where the line crosses itself on a corner's inside it cuts the corner. At a junction it goes straight on, or the way the player looks when the look turns from straight on; it never makes a U-turn, takes an alley only where nothing else goes on, and ends at a dead end. It says where each junction with a choice is and whether it ends at a dead end. There are no lane changes: the plan's push-back. While the roads round an ask aren't in yet, the worker holds it, says `wait`, and answers when they come (the review's m21).
- **The host** (`Curb.ts`, `life.curb` and `life.curbRoll`):
  - `curb.land(x, z, heading, speed?)` asks the worker. `curb.state` goes 'asking', then 'on', or 'none' (FIND A STREET); `curb.waiting` is true while the worker waits for the roads, not yet rather than no street. With `speed` the bus rolls on from it and eases to the pace (H1).
