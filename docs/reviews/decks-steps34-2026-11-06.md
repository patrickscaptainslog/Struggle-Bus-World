## Findings

### Major

**M1. A city config can loosen any QA check with `NaN`, and the report doesn't list it.**
- *Where.* `qa.py:190`: `looser = not isinstance(v, (int, float)) or (v - Q[k]) * TIGHTER[k] < 0`. Every comparison with NaN is false, so the value isn't counted as looser and is taken. Python's `json.load` (`qa.py` `run()`) reads `NaN` in a config file without complaint.
- *Failing case (ran).* `"qa": {"unsupportedMax": NaN, "standInsMax": NaN, "streetFigureMin": NaN}` all come back taken, and `refused` is `[]`. After that, `0.9 > nan` is False and `0.1 < nan` is False. So `unsupported`, every bridge check, and in step 6 the whole gate can never be over, whatever the bake draws. `qacheck.py`'s `OVERRIDES` has no case for it.
- *Fix.* Refuse anything that isn't a finite real number: `not isinstance(v, (int, float)) or isinstance(v, bool) or not math.isfinite(v) or ...`. Add `('unsupportedMax', float('nan'), False)` and `('streetFigureMin', float('nan'), False)` to `OVERRIDES`.

**M2. Since step 4, `deckFreeEnd` lets off nearly every deck end: a kept way within 12 m excuses an end at any height.**
- *Where.* `qa.py:389-401` (`free_end`): an end counts as free, and so is measured, only when there's no kept way, deck or building within 12 m. Nothing compares the end's top with the way's ground. Step 4 brings back each bridge's own approach at grade, ending where the bridge's range ends. So every deck end that meets its own road now has a kept way within 12 m.
- *Failing case (from the code; not run, the extract isn't here).* A hero deck that stops 30 m up, right over its restored approach, reads "a kept way within 12 m" and adds nothing to the value. The doc's "`deckFreeEnd` from 2.74 m to 0.00 m (the Golden Gate's Presidio end meets a restored way)" is this exemption at work. The end wasn't measured as landing. Once step 6 sets FAIL, this check can only fail for an end with no road at all.
- *Fix.* Measure every end that isn't shared with another deck or inside a building: top minus the ground there, a kept way or not. Or measure top minus the kept way's own height at its nearest point. Keep the way only as the reason a landing is expected. Say in BRIDGES.md step 4 that the 0.00 m is an exemption, not a landing.

### Minor

**m1. A stretch at grade with no name rule of its own takes the segment's primary name, which is often the bridge's or tunnel's.**
- *Where.* `osm.py:223-237` (`stretch_name` returns the whole-segment rule or None) and `osm.py:506` (`... or rec[2]`, the primary).
- *Case.* The primary is "Golden Gate Bridge", `name_rules` holds only a ranged common rule "Golden Gate Bridge" over the bridge's stretch, and there's no rule for the approach. The approach is then signed "Golden Gate Bridge", which is the very case step 4 set out to fix. `segcheck.py`'s name case gives every stretch its own ranged rule, so it never reaches the fallback. I couldn't count how many San Francisco stretches reach it: the extract isn't on this machine.
- *Fix.* When the primary equals a ranged rule's value and that rule doesn't hold f, give the stretch no name (or `whole` if there is one). Add a segcheck case for it, and log how many stretches fall back.

**m2. `_joined` mirrors a reversed deck's edges.**
- *Where.* `bridges.py:410-415`. When a or b is reversed, `left` and `right` are only flipped end to end. Reversing a line flips its normal, so they should become `-right[::-1]` and `-left[::-1]`.
- *Case.* A deck from (0,0) to (100,0) with `left = -2` and `right = +8` covers z from -2 to +8. Reversed, it should be `left = -8`, `right = +2`. The code keeps -2 and +8, so it covers -8 to +2. The width stays right; an off-centre deck is drawn on the wrong side of its line. After `recentre` the edges are nearly symmetric, so the error is small on today's joins. It wasn't measured.
- *Fix.* Swap and negate, and add a qacheck case that joins an asymmetric deck end to end.

**m3. With the gate set to `fail`, a check that is NOT READ passes, so a city whose `bridges.py` throws passes all fifteen.**
- *Where.* `qa.py:719` counts only `over`. `failed()` (in `bridge_checks`) turns every check NOT READ when the extract or the plan throws. The report says "NOT READ: never a pass", but at step 6 it is one.
- *Fix.* Settle this before step 6. Either a NOT READ whose `why` starts "failed to read" fails a `fail` gate, or the summary lists NOT READ keys beside `failed` and `bake.py` treats them as failures.

**m4. `atGradeLoss` only sees segments flagged `is_bridge`, so it can't catch a bad cut on a level, covered or under-construction stretch.**
- *Where.* `qa.py:576-589`: `flagged` takes only `'is_bridge' in fl`. Step 4 also brings back stretches cut by `level_rules`, `is_covered` and `is_under_construction` (`osm.py:80`, `OFF_GROUND`), and no check measures their road at grade.
- *Fix.* Rasterize each off-the-ground segment's stretches the rules leave at grade (`off_ground`/`at_grade` again), and measure what isn't drawn there. Or say in section 4 that the check covers bridges only.

### Nits

- **n1.** `osm.py:183`: a range end of 1 + 2e-9 makes the rule cover the whole segment, so the whole segment is left out. Clamp with a wider tolerance (1e-6) instead of falling back to all of it.
- **n2.** `bridges.py:606`: `water_run` counts a run of k wet samples as k x STEP. The samples are L/(n-1) apart, which is under STEP, and k samples span k-1 gaps. So a crossing reads up to 10 m long, which moves the S cut (55 m) and the height rule a little.
- **n3.** `bridges.py:745`: `through_towers` tapers the pull over 190 m past the outer tower. With the outer tower within 190 m of a deck end, that end moves sideways, and an end shared within 4 m can stop being shared. On San Francisco's XL decks the ends are far enough out (inferred, not run).
- **n4.** `qa.py:270`: until `tower_off` runs, `towerOffDeck`'s limit is the multiplier 1.0 with unit m. A NOT READ line in the report shows "<= 1.00 m".
- **n5.** `osm.py:452-453`: `ranged` keys only on `road_flag_rules`. An extract with that column but not `rail_flag_rules`, `level_rules` or `name_rules` would raise an AttributeError on `row.rail_flag_rules`. `overture.py` writes all four together, so this is only robustness.
- **n6.** `tools/sky/lamps.ts:47,59,106` still load `lamps.bin?v=osm3`. The fetch there is a stub, so nothing breaks, but a grep for the data version finds it.
