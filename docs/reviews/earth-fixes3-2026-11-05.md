## Majors

**1 (major). Folds run out of order when one waits on a stored picture, so an older blast is kept over a newer one.** `src/world/GeoPaint.ts:678`. Found by the pictures lens and the phone lens on their own.

Any device: it's the code, not the GPU. Over ground painted before and kept (after a reload, or once its pictures have closed: `OPEN` keeps 96 idle ones, and one MEGA opens about that many), the player lets go of a MEGA and breathes onto its splat while the MEGA is still being drawn in. On screen the breath is on top. After a reload, a new map, or any tile copied out again, the MEGA's hues are on top wherever the breath reached pictures the MEGA hadn't drawn yet: the touch-up is gone there, whole or cut along a row of pictures, a straight east-west seam. Coverage is a max, so nothing goes grey; only the breath's hues go. A hide in that window does the same through the journal, and so, rarely, does a boot.
- How: `work()` (676-683) moves on to the next fold only when the current one waits: `if (!this.foldStep(f, end)) { k++; continue; }`. `foldStep` returns false when `pic()` (622-630) finds the picture in the index but not open (363), and `load()` (639-657) is one async IndexedDB get, so the picture can't come before the next frame's `work()`. With folds [MEGA, breath], the MEGA's wait gives the breath the rest of the frame, and the breath can draw pictures that come later in the MEGA's walk, which `picsOver` builds row by row, south to north (505-517). A full MEGA from 600 m (r 468, reach 1.6r + 4 = 753 m) walks about 121 pictures and waits a frame at each closed one; a breath (r 65) reaches 2 to 9 and folds in a frame or two. When the MEGA gets there, `drawSplat` (442-444) runs `d[t] = Math.max(old, cov); if (cov >= 128 || cov > old) d[t + 1] = out.hue * 32 + 16;`, so the MEGA's hue replaces the breath's wherever its cover is 128 or more, and its interior is cov 255: every texel they share there. The live tiles stack in time order (`GroundPaint.put`'s deferred over-paint is applied at `land`, 1112-1118 and 636-641), and nothing writes a tile back to the pictures: the screen and the store disagree, and the store keeps the MEGA on top for good. `GroundPaint`'s own jobs keep paint in order with `under` (its comment at 199-206); folds have nothing like it.
- Through the journal: `main.ts` 863-866 runs `persist` on `visibilitychange` (an app switch or a lock) as well as `pagehide`. `persist` (786-797) journals the waiting MEGA whole at its first closed picture and finishes the breath into open pictures, which are journalled as pictures; `start` (275-276) adds the MEGA again after putting them, so it's drawn over every later blast in its footprint, even those that folded in order. It shows if the page is killed while hidden.
- Live, in the same session: a tile made again while the MEGA's fold waits (detach, `keepShown`, then `attachGeo`) gets its mask, then `pendingOver` replays only the pending MEGA over the finished breath. And `keepShown` and `paintCopied` hold 64 tiles (`GroundPaint.ts` 846, 863), so after flying over other painted ground `attachGeo` (799-802) copies a tile out of the pictures again; a new map clears `prepared`.
- At boot: `add()` can queue folds before `ready`, and the journal's blasts are added after them (276). Real, but rare: a blast has to land before the store's index read and the journal's put are done.
- The skeptics. The firehose doesn't do it: every hose impact and the closer share one `SuperSplat.bands` (473-476, passed at 582 and 584), so the same layout, middle, ring and hue (`Splat.ts` 229-230, 258-262), and `bandHue` reads only the point (`blastLayout` 112-116), so the hue at a texel is the same whichever is drawn last. A later fold also gets the frame only when every fold before it waits, so a breath behind 49 pending hose folds is less likely to go first than one behind a lone MEGA. On how soon a breath can follow a MEGA they differ: one reads `fireMega`'s "a mega needs no full belly" as a breath at once (one that lands about 0.3 s after the MEGA finds it still in its southern rows); the other reads `Flight.tryBarf`'s duration (1.0 s) and recharge (1.6 s) as the next tap 2.6 s after the let-go. On that reading it needs a fold that outlasts 2.6 s: a big MEGA, over old paint whose pictures are mostly closed (normal play: `close()`, 718-723, keeps 96 by painted time, and `load()` gives a picture it reads its old time, 650, so the old pictures just read round the bus are the first to close); on the A15 at 30 fps (at 60 fps, 134 reads of a frame each are done in about 2.2 s, so it needs reads longer than a frame, which they likely are, queued behind the fold's own writes each second; not measured); and the breath in rows the fold hasn't reached yet.
- Measured by the phone lens's skeptic, in Node (the real `GroundPaint` and `GeoPaint` over `MemoryStore`, a read answered by the next frame, the fastest case; scratch, not in the repo): 81 breaths laid over 1.6 km of ground, 154 pictures stored, a reload, 18 pictures open. A full MEGA from 600 m (134 pictures) waited on reads 116 times and took 173 frames, 5.8 s at 30 fps. A breath 2 frames after it drew first in all 6 shared pictures, and after a reload 0 of 1,233 texels had its hue (1,225 had the MEGA's); a breath after the fold was done, 1,233 of 1,233. At 78 frames (2.6 s at 30 fps) the fold was at picture 67-73 of 134: a breath 300 m north, ahead of the fold, kept 0 of 1,221; at the middle and 300 m south, all of theirs. A hide in between: 0 of 1,233. A tile made again in between: 0 of 1,858. Not checked: IndexedDB's real latency on a phone or under load, the firehose's closer followed by a breath, anything in a browser.
- Tests: nothing paints two overlapping blasts with a wait between them. `paint-check`'s budget row is one MEGA alone, and `keep.mjs`'s heavy row (117-180) is 200 breaths over about 18 pictures, all opened in the session, so nothing ever waits.
- Fix: keep folds in order: stop at the first that waits (or let a waiting fold hold the later ones that share a picture with it), and read a fold's stored pictures in one get before it starts, which makes 116 waits about one (28). In `persist`, journal whole every fold after the first one kept whole (or every one that shares a picture with it), instead of finishing it. At boot, queue the journal's blasts first. `attachGeo`'s replays rely on the same order.
- Major: `keep.mjs`'s own bar on the hue (90 %) would fail those texels, and the producer's ruling is that the paint stays. It comes from a natural pattern, a MEGA and then a breath, over the player's own ground; one skeptic puts it at the low end of major, narrower than M1. Confidence high on the mechanism, traced line by line; medium on how often, worked out from the config.

**2 (major). A failing IndexedDB is taken for a full store, then for a blocked one, and its connection is never opened again: what's painted after that is lost at the next boot.** `src/world/GeoPaint.ts:763` and `885`. Found by the pictures lens and the phone lens on their own.

iPhone Safari on the Moon (`?moon`, or the trip once down). The player paints, switches to another app for a while and comes back, and iOS has dropped the page's IndexedDB connection (WebKit's "Connection to Indexed Database server lost. Refresh the page to try again", a known iOS failure after time in the background). Every get, put and remove on the kept connection fails, and within a second the budget is cut twice and the store goes to memory.
- With 64 MB held, failed writes 1 and 2 cut the budget to 51.2 MB, then 36.9, and the writes after each evict down to 46.1, then 33.2: the oldest 28 %, then 48 %, leave the index in memory. The third swaps in the memory `Fallback` at 16 MB, and the next write evicts to 14.4 MB, 22.5 % kept. That's three frames and their round trips, well under a second once anything is coded. With the connection dead the removes fail too, so IndexedDB still has those pictures at the next boot; and since reads fail too, all the old paint that isn't open already is out of view for the session, not 78 % of it. The 22.5 % needs 64 MB held, about four hours of painting; at 15 MB held the two cuts take 48 % out, and the fallback's 16 MB doesn't come into it.
- Old paint not in memory doesn't show on tiles that come in: their pictures open blank. New paint goes to memory only. The first memory write that finds nothing pending deletes the journal the last hide left, and at the next close everything painted since the switch is gone but the last second. Next session the old paint is back and the session's isn't.
- A picture opened blank and painted, or evicted this session and painted again, and still dirty at the last hide, is journalled with the time now and only the new paint: at the next boot it replaces that picture's old paint in IndexedDB for good.
- Any one failed write that isn't a full disk cuts the budget to 0.8 of what's kept, for the rest of the session (only `fails` resets). If IndexedDB works again, the next write's remove deletes the oldest ~28 % of the player's paint for good, and each later lone failure ~28 % more, down to a floor of 1 MB: about 4 minutes at Jet by `BUDGET`'s comment (15 MB an hour), the oldest evicted as they paint. That's M1's loss again.
- How: `IdbStore.open` (884-895), `this.db ??= new Promise(...)`, is one connection a page, kept even once it's rejected or closed: no `onclose`, `onversionchange` or `onblocked`, no retry, and nothing in `src/` reads an error's name. On a lost connection `db.transaction` throws inside `tx()`'s executor (900), so every call rejects. `write()`'s failure handler (759-768) never looks at the error: `if (++this.fails < 3) this.budget = Math.max(BUDGET / 64, Math.min(this.budget, this.bytes * 0.8)); else if (!(this.store instanceof Fallback)) { this.store = new Fallback(this.store); this.budget = BUDGET / 4; this.fails = 0; }`, and the budget is never raised again (181, 763). `work()` writes again on the next frame because `this.bytes > this.budget` (693), and that write evicts (728-738). `Fallback.get` (974-983) swallows the failed read and returns nothing, so `load()` (643-652) opens a blank picture under the stored time. The success path (747-757) removes the journal whichever store took the rows, and `persist()` (799-804) journals only what's sent, coded and dirty, never `Fallback.mem`. `start()` (249, 255-259) keeps a journal row over the stored picture when it's newer by time and puts it whole; `merged()` only joins journal rows with the old build's, never with the stored code.
- Worse with a store that deletes but won't put (a full disk): `Fallback`'s 16 MB counts the whole index, IndexedDB's old paint with it, and `Fallback.remove` (989-992) passes evictions on (`void this.was.remove(keys)`). Going to the fallback with 64 MB held deletes all the oldest paint past 14.4 MB from IndexedDB for good, against "reading the older paint from IndexedDB still" in b1b8a36's message and in DECISIONS. 10 is the same handler under a quota.
- And a write that never settles leaves `writing` true: the flush at 667 never runs again, nothing is written, and `close()` never runs, so every picture opened stays open, 64 KB each. No trigger is shown for that: WebKit aborts or refuses transactions on a lost connection, so they settle.
- The same root, smaller: at boot, when `start()`'s put fails (258-262), the journal is still removed or written again empty, and the old build's pictures are deleted (11). And the eviction (731) doesn't spare dirty open pictures, while the index's time lags the picture's until it's coded: after a cut, the next write can shut an old picture repainted in the last second, new paint and all.
- Tests: `paint-check`'s blocked row refuses everything from boot, reads too, so its index is empty and there's nothing to lose; its quota row refuses only puts past 3 MB. Nothing fails a store that already holds paint, mid-session.
- What softens it: on the Moon's page a lost GL context reloads the page (`main.ts:1009`), and the reload opens a new connection and goes through the journal, which works. So the dead connection needs Safari's network process gone while the page and its GL context live on. How often isn't measured, and the producer's iPhone 17 Pro is unlikely to show it. The other half's example, a write aborted while the page is hidden, is unlikely: `work()` runs only from `GroundPaint.flush` in the frame loop (`main.ts` 1150, 1664), so no write starts while hidden, and a write of a few rows takes milliseconds.
- Fix: drop `db` on any failure and on `close`, and open it again once before counting a failure. That may not be enough on iOS: two WebKit bugs report the same error on reopening until the page is refreshed, [197050](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=197050) (iOS 12, the network process dying while Safari is suspended) and [277615](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=277615) (2024, iOS 17-18, after hours in the background); the skeptic read summaries of these, not the threads, and WebKit's behaviour here is from knowledge, not tested. So the real fix is the journal: keep it while in the fallback, at a hide journal the fallback's rows too, newest first, as far as `localStorage` allows, and keep a picture dirty or journalled until a store that lasts has it. Cut the budget only on `QuotaExceededError`, or, since WebKit may report a full disk as `UnknownError`, on failures that repeat after a fresh open. When a read fails, leave the picture unopened (`undefined`), not blank, or merge journal rows with the stored picture at boot. In the fallback, don't pass evictions on.
- Major, not a blocker: it needs a browser fault, and with a dead connection most old paint comes back at the next boot. But kept paint is the producer's ruling, and the fault recurs on the game's own platform: rare in a session, not across many, as the third review rated M3. Confidence high on the code, medium on how often iOS does it.

**3 (major). Each copy that lands rebuilds its coarse region and copies the region's painted tiles again, and painted ground can go bare for seconds.** `src/world/GroundPaint.ts:544`. The phone lens filed it major and the pictures lens minor, on their own; its skeptic held it at major.

Any device, worst on an A15 at 30 fps. On the Moon a player boots near, or flies back at Jet over, ground painted in an earlier session (M4's strip). A painted region comes in coarse and bare, since the merged ground carries no paint, and its painted tiles pop in one at a time as their copies land, where DECISIONS says "nothing pops in". Each copy that lands for a tile not yet split out rebuilds the whole region's ground in the next update: up to 16 times for a fully painted region, where 688c9de built it once. Each rebuild queues the region's tiles already showing their paint to be copied again, 2n - 1 copies for n tiles, and each of those merges nothing but uploads a whole 64 KB mask again. Copies run first in the 2 ms, so these starve the folds and the store's writes, and paint in the coarse ring comes in at about half the rate. Painting from up high is worse: every breath that reaches a tile of a coarse region not yet split out rebuilds it and copies all its painted tiles again, up to 15 x ~2 ms (Node) for a breath whose own fold is ~4-5 ms.
- What makes it major, its skeptic says: a copy queued again for a tile already showing its paint makes `copyingTile()` true, and `detach` skips `keepShown` while it is (528, from 448aa6c). At the region's swap to detail, `swapRegion` (`City.ts:1469-1470`) drops the tile's ground, `detach` throws its mask away, `attachGeo` (from `fitTileTerrain`) finds no copy waiting, and `want()` returns early because the tile is still copying. So the detailed tile comes in bare until the new copy lands: 448aa6c's "a tile made again keeps it" is broken for every split tile with a copy queued. A fully painted region needs 31 copies, about 0.5 s on this laptop at 60 fps and 2-3 s on an A15 at 30 fps if a copy takes 2-3 frames of the share (`GeoPaint.ts:36`'s "two or three times Node"), and the region stays coarse about 2-3 s before it swaps to detail at 520 m. Likely at Jet over painted ground, and on a dive from above about 940 m to under 550 m right after painting from up high. Painted ground ahead of the bus would go bare for up to a few seconds on a slow phone, in M4's own case.
- How: for a tile that isn't loaded, `paintCopied` (844-848) sets `this.prepared.set(key, d)` and calls `this.onPaintReady?.(...)`, which is `City.ts:557` `setOwnGround`: the region goes into `groundStale` (1406), and the next `update()` (1065-1068) runs `buildRegionGround`. That asks `this.ground.hasSaved(tx, tz)` of every tile with terrain, split out or not (1333), and `hasSaved` (543-545) is `if (this.prepared.has(key)) return true; this.pics.want(key, ox, oz);`. But `attachGeo` deleted the entry when the tile took its paint (804), and `copyStep` cleared `copying` (`GeoPaint.ts:614`), so `want()`, whose only guard is `if (this.copying.has(key)) return;` (`GeoPaint.ts:549`), queues a whole new copy with a fresh 64 KB array (586). Its result takes `paintCopied`'s loaded branch (829-841): merged under the mask, its whole rect set and queued for upload (837-841) whether or not anything changed. Traced by hand, first in first out: the rebuild for tile k queues tiles 1 to k-1 again where they're not queued already.
- A rebuild's cost: the merged mesh made again (`takeGrid`, `City.ts:1603-1608`, uses the worker's grid once, then `makeHeightGrid` on the main thread, so rebuild k makes the 8 m grids of the 16 - k tiles still merged again: up to 15 + 14 + ... + 1 = 120 grids of 33 x 33 vertices for one region), the region's 259 x 259 map texture with mips made again from the same bytes (`moonBoot.ts:281-284`), and a new material. On the third review's let-go (67-150 ms for 43 `setOwnGround` calls) that's ~0.5 ms a rebuild, ~0.1 ms per tile still merged and ~0.3 ms for the tile split out: 1.5-2.3 ms with 7-15 left, outside the 2 ms. The skeptic of 9 timed a 15-tile rebuild in Node: 1.9 ms median, 2.6 p90, 3.6 the most, before uploading the new ~600 KB buffer and the mipmapped map. A copy is ~2 ms in Node: the old 2.6 ms less its string key, plus a second cos a texel for `cols(i)` in `locate()`.
- On frame time alone it's less than major, its skeptic says: a stale region is rebuilt at most once a frame, as every breath at altitude already paid at 688c9de and the fix accepted, about 2-4 ms on this laptop by the let-go's numbers, and the copies stay inside their 2 ms. Nothing has measured it: no `paint-check` row runs a City (`tiles` calls `hasSaved` once a tile and never attaches), and City's share isn't timed anywhere. A copy for a tile already gone is never dropped either (15).
- Fix: `want()` and `hasSaved` skip a tile that's loaded with its paint (or `hasSaved` answers true for one), or `detach` keeps what a tile shows unless the copy queued is the tile's first; split a region's painted tiles out together once its queued copies are in; keep the region's map texture across rebuilds. On the GPU, `gpu/m4-attach.mjs`'s `copySteps` for a tile's key after its attach shows the copies made again; time `City.update` in those frames.
- Confidence high on the mechanism, medium on how often on an A15, which is unmeasured.

**4 (major). M2's let-go: each touched region is rebuilt once now, but every touched tile still makes its own ground in that one frame.** `src/world/City.ts:550`. The phone lens.

Any device, the A15 at 30 fps most. A MEGA from 600 m over unpainted coarse ground, the third review's own case. At the let-go `begin()` marks the blast's ~43-49 tiles as their own ground, and the next `City.update()` rebuilds the 4-9 regions once each: that's the fix. But in the same frame each tile newly split out makes its mesh, its map texture and its material and is attached, and the next `work()` makes its 64 KB mask. By the old measurement's own numbers that frame is ~17-25 ms of extra work on this laptop's page (down from 67-150 ms), not the few ms M2's Done asked for: a dropped frame on an A15 at 30 fps. Every breath from ~500 m up over coarse ground pays the same for its 9-16 tiles, ~4-13 ms.
- How: `City.ts:550-555`, `onTilesTouched = (keys) => { for (const k of keys) { ...; this.setOwnGround(tx, tz); } }`, is called by `GroundPaint.begin` (584-587) at the let-go, before `city.update` in `main.ts`'s frame, with `reach = spec.r * Math.max(spec.reach, ...) + 4`: r 468 gives 753 m, a box of 1,506 m, 7 x 7 tiles 88 % of the time (49 tiles 78 %, 42 21 %). Only loaded coarse tiles split; the third review's GPU runs had 24-43 attaching. `setOwnGround` (1399-1407) only marks the region stale, and the next `update` (1065-1069) rebuilds every stale region at once, with no budget. In `buildRegionGround` (1334-1339) each tile of its own not yet at `terrainStep` 4 gets `dropTileTerrain` and `makeTileTerrain(lt, 4)`: a new grid on the main thread (`takeGrid`, 1604-1608, used up the worker's at the region's first build), a new 67 x 67 map texture cut from the region's, with mipmaps (`moonTileMap`, `moonMaterial.ts:327-331`), a new `ShaderMaterial` (`makeMoonGround`), and an attach (`want`, `picsOver`). Later in the same `update()`, `ground.work()` starts the MEGA's job: its prepare (about 3 ms by `GeoPaint.add`'s own note, not counted here) and `mask()` for every loaded tile in the box, a 64 KB array and texture each (`GroundPaint.ts` 914-917, 977-993).
