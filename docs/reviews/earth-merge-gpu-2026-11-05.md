# The merge on the GPU: the whole trip, the lanes and the night (feat/earth, 24e3747)

The first account, 2026-11-05, on the laptop's GPU: what the second account asked for once `main` was merged into `feat/earth` (788f88a, a4fa3c3; `docs/TODO.md`, "Merge `main` into `feat/earth`"): "(`?earth&fps`, then `?moonland&fps`): the whole trip with the new controls, street to Moon and back; on the street the bigger lane, and on the way to the Moon the same lane; `?earth` at night (`&t=2026-11-05T06:00:00Z`): the stars and the Moon from the street, up through 11-15 km where the flight's sky comes in, and back down, where the street's night table must be there at once, not seconds later; `?moonland`: no San Francisco sky on the Moon".

The runs began on c86bad1 and moved to 24e3747 when it landed (review four's 5 to 7: the space sky on rows of its own, the street's table kept at the hand-up, the live sky held while the flight has the frame, no opening's gate on the way home), since those are what the night ask is about. Everything below is 24e3747's unless it says c86bad1 or e0c89d8. While they ran, `feat/earth` moved on to e0c89d8 (review four's other fixes: the Moon's paint, a press ended at the hand-overs, a Moon reached on Earth's page). Its `src/` diff from 24e3747 touches none of the palette's writers, the resolve or `StreetSky`, so the findings below stand there by reading; the trip, the flicks and the night were run again on it (end of this file). Line numbers are e0c89d8's.

**Verdict.** Every ask passes but one half of one. The whole trip with the new controls, street to Moon and back: pass. The bigger lane on the street: pass. The same lane on the way to the Moon: pass. No San Francisco sky on the Moon: pass. One major, four minors and the tools' notes, below.

## How it ran

- `git archive 24e3747` into `../sb-src-earth24` (outside the repo, `node_modules` a junction to this worktree's), `npx --no-install vite build` into `../sb-build-earth24` in 1.0 s, only the chunk-size warning (main chunk 1,045.15 kB, 330.69 kB gzip), served by `tools/sky/serve-dist.mjs` on 4179. c86bad1's build for its warm-up check and one night run; e0c89d8's (main chunk 1,047.59 kB) on 4181 for the reruns. Every server and browser started here was stopped.
- Edge headless on the Intel UHD 620, real GPU, 393 x 852 at 1x unless a tool says 3x, a fixed step of 1/30 s where the tool has one. Each browser ran alone and started only with 1 GiB or more free: `/proc/meminfo`'s MemFree, the counter `FreePhysicalMemory` reads, at 1.05 to 1.58 GiB at the starts, and `FreePhysicalMemory` itself a moment later, with its own PowerShell running, at 0.93 to 1.48 GiB. Between runs the laptop sat at 0.84 to 0.96 GB, with other jobs on it. So every real-time number here is this laptop's under load; frame counts, programs, draws and pixels aren't.
- The night's checks needed a tool that reads the palette every frame, so this account wrote three, kept with the frames and not in the repo (they can come if wanted): `tools/earth/handback.mjs` (the street at the asked hour; a full pull in Turbo 3 with frames at 1 to 14.6 km and every frame through the hand-up; a push down with every frame through the hand-back, 90 frames level after it and 3 s of the wall clock; then on up to TO THE MOON?, the Moon's page, home and `?earth&resume`'s hand-back the same way. Each frame read: the palette's street rows, 0-49 and 56-63, and its space rows, 50-55, hashed; NIGHT_SKY's and FOG's colours; the live sky's `held`, table key and light; `StreetSky`'s stars and Moon), `moonlook.mjs` (the Moon from the street with a real finger's look up) and `progs.mjs` (every shader the page makes, and when, against the warm-up's rounds).

## Findings

**M1 (major). On the way home at night the flight draws Earth in the day's colours, and at 14 km the street's night turns 90 % of the picture in one frame.** `src/sky/moonrun.ts:270`, `src/main.ts:147`. New in 24e3747 (review four's 7); c86bad1 didn't have it.

Any device, every trip home from the Moon (`?earth&resume`) when the street's live table isn't the day's: at night, in the blue hour, at sunset and golden hour, and a little at noon, whose table isn't the day's bytes either (read; only the night was run). From the page change in the long dark until the street takes the bus at 14 km, the plain Earth's land, its air and its lights are in `dayRows`' colours; the street's first frame then draws them in the night's.
- Measured (`handback.mjs`, 06:00Z, its home leg): on the resume page the street's rows hash 0a0b73ca92e737c5, the day's, from the boot to the last flight frame, and 1b59973e92e737c5, the night table the one page has, from the street's first; FOG reads #6e7984, then #262455. In the frames the ground's unlit pixels are [26,32,41] and the sea's [15,19,26] in the flight (`home-a00`, `home-a01`), and [12,17,48] and [10,13,41] in the first frame the street draws (`home-b01`): 301,061 of 334,836 pixels change, where the two frames before it changed 57,337 and 60,054 and the one-page hand-back's same frame 101,050. Over the same place at the same hour the one-page flight draws the land [12,17,48] (`down-a03`): one Earth, two colours by the page it's on.
- c86bad1, the same run on its build: the resume page had the night table from its boot (the live sky wrote it, unheld), and its home hand-back changed 115,123 pixels. Its one-page hand-back was the jump instead, 263,263 pixels (the day's world rows written at the hand-up, which 24e3747 fixed). So 24e3747 took the jump off the one page and put it on the way home.
- How: `moonrun.ts:270`, `if (flightFirst) takeFrame(true)`, writes `dayRows` with `SPACE_ROWS` on `?earth&resume`; `main.ts:147` holds the live sky from the resume boot until EarthLanding's `enter` (1700, `held = false` and `repaint()`); and the space branch still draws on the street's rows: `skyResolve.ts:316` the air (FOG, SKY_WARM), `:384` green land (GREEN_GREY), `:415-416` the lights (LAMP). On the one page the street's table is on those rows the whole way, as 24e3747 keeps it at the hand-up, so there Earth is the night's.
- Fix: on `?earth&resume`, write the live sky's table once at the boot before holding it (a `repaint()` and one `update()` ahead of `held = true`), so the flight's world rows are the street's as on the one page; or give the space branch's land, air and lights rows of their own beside 50-55, which also keeps them out of whatever the street writes. Check: `handback.mjs`'s home rows, the street rows' hash in the flight equal to the street's, and `home-b01`'s change no bigger than `down-b01`'s.
- Major: it's the frame the ask names, on the way home, every night, and the cut the producer ruled out ("I don't want the screen to skip jump"). This morning's report says nothing jumps in colour at 15 km, which holds; this is 14 km, going the other way.

**m1 (minor). On the way home the street's stars come in after the street takes the bus, from none.** `src/sky/StreetSky.ts:262`, `:365`. From the merge: c86bad1 has it too.
- Measured (`handback.mjs`, the home leg): `StreetSky` draws 0 stars, its limit at -2, from the street's first frame (`home -01`) for 8 frames; then the limit climbs from -1.99 to 6.5 over 48 frames (1.6 s, `LAND_S`), to 8,797 stars at +56. On the one page the same hand-back draws 8,797 from its first frame. c86bad1: 0 until +10, then the same fade.
- How: the files' landing is stamped on `globals.uTime` (262) and the fade (365) runs on it. On the resume page the flight writes `uTime` from its own clock (`moonrun.ts:554`) while the street boots behind it, and the street writes its own `simTime` back (`main.ts:1251`) only once it has the bus, so the stars wait until the street's clock passes the flight's at the landing, then fade in. In this run the flight's clock had only the tool's few frames on it, the rest being skipped, so the wait was 8 frames; flown, it would be as long as the flight had flown on the resume page when the files landed, seconds (read, not measured).
- Fix: on a street booted behind the flight, stamp the landing on the street's own clock, or skip the fade (`land = 1`). Check: the home rows' `stars.drawn` at the first street frame equal to the one page's.
- Minor: in a dive the sky is a strip at the top, as in these frames; coming in shallow, the night sky is empty for those seconds, then fills brightest first.
