# Fog City: Struggle Bus World, season 1

## The producer's ask (2026-11-20)

"please use the read.me screenshots and general idea of the game to create a live game description i can use to better build this game. be bold and wow me with what this game could look like. i think the biggest part we're missing is a theme/premise/reason to come back. please do your best with this trailer to figure that out and paint a picture of how cool it could be. we need to figure out how to have a cool dynamic between buses/factions. right now, if you want your neighbourhood to float, you have to hit it a bunch of times, and you're essentially standing still while you're doing that so that doesn't make so much sense - its not good gameplay. idk. i think we need to get extreme here and think way outside the box and think big. any ideas?"

Behind it: the game's first outside player, a nine-year-old, loved shooting the other buses most and asked for upgrades and different guns (TODO.md, 2026-11-18).

## How it was made

Four creative directors pitched blind, one lens each: theme first, the nine-year-old, movement, the live game. All four landed on the same premise on their own: a grey fog that drains the city, kids in school buses, houses that float because colour is lighter than air, a daily reset with an overnight raid, the Moon as the finale. This proposal is that premise, with the best mechanic from each pitch.

## In one line

**Lasso a real block, watch it float, and tow the houses home while the other buses try to shoot them off your tail.**

Tagline: *Everybody's on the struggle bus. Ours flies.*

## The three ideas

1. **The Fog.** Every evening, on the real clock, the marine layer rolls in under the Golden Gate and greys whatever is still on the ground. Only what floats survives. A player's floating school is their save game, and it grows every visit.
2. **Loops.** The bus trails a ribbon of paint. Close a loop and everything inside bursts into colour; close it fast and it lifts off the ground. Nobody stops moving, and hovering takes nothing.
3. **The tail.** Lifted houses hitch to the bus on strings, like balloons. Tow them home to bank them. Every house on a tail is a target, and every bridge is a knife.

## The premise

One evening the Fog came in under the Golden Gate and never fully left. It takes the colour out of everything it touches: the Painted Ladies, the cable cars, the people. Grown-ups go grey and still, staring at grey phones. Kids are immune, and one colour can't be taken: school-bus yellow (its official name is National School Bus Glossy Yellow). So the kids took the buses. The buses fly, and they barf paint.

Paint is colour, and colour is life: a painted person dances, a painted picnic starts up again (the ruling "life follows paint" is already this). Colour is also lighter than air: paint a block joyful enough and it lets go of the ground and floats above the fog line, where the Fog can't reach. Five crews took to the sky, each sure the city should wear its colours. They fight the Fog, and each other harder. Every evening the Fog comes back. Whatever is on the ground goes grey; whatever floats survives.

It gives every rule the game has a reason (the grey city, paint as the only colour, the Lift, the school bus), it is the city's own weather and its most famous picture (the bridge's towers above the fog), and every city has a Grey of its own (Lagos's harmattan haze, Tokyo's rainy season, London's pea-souper).

## Loops: taking the city at full speed

Today a block takes four counted hits on one 128 m square, at least 5 s apart, so a player hovers and waits. That rule goes.

- **The ribbon.** Flying low, the bus trails a ribbon of paint over the roofs and streets below; the faster and lower, the thicker. Above about 300 m, or slower than Cruise, it paints nothing.
- **The snap.** Crossing your own ribbon closes the loop: everything inside blooms in a wave from the ribbon inward, with a chord and the street names.
- **A loop paints; a fast loop lifts.** Closed at Express or faster, the squares inside lift. One block at Express is about 8 s; six blocks at Jet float a neighbourhood.
- **The streak.** Each loop closed without dropping below Express makes the next bigger. A dive from height starts one; flying under a bridge refills it.
- **The tap still fires.** A Comet ahead stitches a loop's last gap shut; a Lob over a hill closes a loop that can't be flown.
- **It must feel like drifting a kart.** Loops snap to the real street graph, corners pull the bus in, a gap under 40 m closes itself, and a ghost line shows the loop before it shuts. The squares (128 m) stay the unit that lifts, as the areas engine has them.
- **The snip.** Rivals lay ribbons too. Flying through an open one shatters it: its paint is the snipper's, and its bus floods and barrel-rolls clear.

## The tail: bus against bus

- **Hitch.** A lifted block's houses float up and hitch to the bus on strings, each with its street name.
- **Bank.** Towed home to the crew's school in the sky, they dock and are safe for good. The school grows with every house.
- **Greed.** Every house adds drag; a long tail is slow, huge and seen from far off. Bank now, or one more block?
- **Snap.** A shell on a rival's tail snaps strings; loose houses drift, and the first bus through keeps them.
- **Burst.** A bus hit by a shell bursts like a piñata of paint over the blocks below, so a fight paints the city in the winner's colours. A head-on at Turbo 3 hands the whole tail to whoever hit harder.
- **Bridges are knives.** A tail that touches a tower or a cable sheds houses.
- **Nobody is knocked out.** A bus that is hit spins, is flung clear (built) and gets 3 s to run. A nearly empty bus flies fastest, so whoever is losing hunts. The crews gang up on whoever holds the most sky.

## Crews

Each crew's ribbon behaves differently. Built-in rivalries: Inferno against Atlantis (fire and water), Hyperglow against the Elderwood (the towers against the parks).

| Crew | Its style | Its ribbon | Its gun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Team Super Rainbow | Steals | Bigger chords; loops spill colour onto the blocks next door; raids everywhere | The Prism: one shot split five ways |
| Atlantis | Floods | Flows downhill and paints the slopes below; hunts the waterfront | The Riptide: a water cannon that cuts tails |
| The Elderwood | Roots | Vines heal small gaps in a loop (the kindest for a new player); its houses are hard to tow away | The Vine: a lasso that plucks one house from 100 m |
| Hyperglow | Blazes | Thinnest and fastest; glows at night, when it raids | The chrome shell: ricochets off towers |
| Inferno | Burns | Burns through any rival ribbon it crosses; rams | The lava Lob: burning puddles that cut ribbons |

**Captains who remember.** Every computer crew has captains with a name, a face on the grille and a memory. A captain who knocks you under the Golden Gate wears your colours as a trophy decal, levels up and sends you a postcard about it; beaten, he comes back with a scorched hood, wanting revenge. Beat a captain, take his gun. All of it lives on the phone, with no server, and it answers the biggest risk: computer buses that feel like bots.

## Guns and upgrades

The eight built shots become guns that are won, and what unlocks each is the kind of building banked, read from the map's own tags, so every city hands out the same guns from its own buildings.

| Bank one of these | Win |
|---|---|
| A fire station | The Hose |
| A harbour, pier or lighthouse | The Torpedo |
| A bell tower or church | The Sonic Boom |
| A water tower | The Waterfall |
| A stadium | The Belly Flop |
| The tallest tower in a neighbourhood | The Comet |
| A school | One more house on the tail |

The school in the sky grows classrooms as houses are banked: Shop (handling, stronger strings, a longer tail), Science (guns: two picked between trips; in flight the gear and the nose pick the shot, as today), Art (looks). Nothing that paints, speeds or protects is ever sold; later, money buys looks only.

## The reason to come back

- **Every day.** The Fog Report: what survived the night and what the other crews did meanwhile ("Inferno took three houses off your school at 2:14 AM. They're over Balboa Park."). With no server, their moves come from the date and the hours that passed, so every phone sees the same city. Golden hour at the real local sunset: paint counts double as the lamps come on, then the foghorn, last call to bank. Today's field trip: one block, the same for everyone, a score to text.
- **Every week.** Crew week: one crew's Principal, a giant articulated bus, roams; beat it for its crew's gun. Heist night on Fridays: a landmark is up for grabs.
- **Every term (about 6 weeks).** Fall, Winter break, Spring, Summer. A report card at the end (grades in Art, PE, Music, Geography), made to be posted. The finale: the biggest school sails to the Moon (the trip is built), and the next term opens a new city.
- **The real calendar.** Fleet Week's jets to paint; Halloween's thick fog with ghost buses; the Dragon Dance at Lunar New Year; an eclipse when the Fog surges; fireworks off every school on New Year's Eve.

## The share

- **Postcards** (the link format is built): a player's school over a real neighbourhood with STEAL ONE, or a friend's street with "I took your street. TAKE IT BACK?" The link lands the friend beside it, against the sender's bus flown by the computer.
- **The report card,** each term.
- **The six-second clip:** a head-on under the Golden Gate, twenty houses scattering; every lift framed for the phone's own screen recording. And the dawn picture: floating schools over a sea of fog, the towers poking through.

## What is built and what is new

Built: the flight (gears, dives, the boom, the 15 km ceiling); eight shots; paint, bloom, chord and street names; rival crews' buses, the shell, the ram, the head-on, being hit and flung; floating houses on a lake of paint and the crews' looks; the real sky on the real clock and the bridges' lights; the Moon trip; the postcard link, the save and the areas engine (on branches).

New: the ribbon, loops and snips; the tail, docking and the school in the sky; the Fog and the Fog Report; captains with memory; guns from buildings; classrooms.

## Three proofs first

Each on an experiment branch, each played by the nine-year-old and five or more other children. Estimates.

1. **The loop** (1 to 2 weeks). One neighbourhood, Alamo Square (rebuilt from the scan on `exp/ladies`): the ribbon, the street-snapped loop, the fast loop that lifts, rivals that snip. The test: does it feel like drifting a kart?
2. **The tail** (1 to 2 weeks). Houses on strings, docking, strings that snap, the bridge that shears, the head-on. The test: do the kids scream, and does an ordinary iPhone hold 30 fps with a long tail?
3. **The Fog** (about 1 week). The fog sea, the evening tide on the real clock, the Fog Report with an overnight raid. The test: do they come back the next day unasked?

Only when all three hold: captains, guns from buildings, classrooms, the crews' ribbons, the postcard's dare, the report card, the Moon finale.

## What it would change (the producer's calls)

- Weather is on hold, and the Fog is weather.
- The takeover rules (STAGES.md 1.3, the floating block's of 2026-11-12: four hits on a square, one every 5 s) go; loops and tails replace them.
- `docs/STUDIO.md`'s spine was the postcard; the postcard becomes the dare, and the Fog and the tail the spine.
- No coins (COINS.md and GRAND-IDEA.md): houses are the currency, and they can be seen.
- A Principal is a bus, not a god, but it is a boss; the gods stay on hold.
- Still no fenced matches: the whole open city, every crew, no lobby.

## What could sink it

- Closing loops at speed with one thumb could feel like parallel parking. Proof 1 decides it.
- Houses on strings with real swing have to read at a glance and hold the frame rate on an A15 in Safari. Proof 2 decides it.
- Computer buses that fly like bots. The captains, and building their brains early, answer it.
